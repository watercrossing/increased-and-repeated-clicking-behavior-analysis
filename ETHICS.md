# Ethics

This is the ethics statement from the paper, reproduced for the artifact evaluation.

> The simulated phishing emails used in this study were part of the organisation's routine
> IT security practices. Employees were not informed in advance about the simulations, as
> such exercises were conducted independently of the research and as part of regular
> security operations. Ethical approval was obtained through a departmental ethics
> application at the institution of the last author. The study was conducted in close
> collaboration with the ISG. The simulated phishing emails used in this study were
> embedded in existing security practices that would have been implemented independently
> of the research. The approved ethics protocol explicitly permitted the use of simulated
> phishing emails without explicit individual consent. To protect participants' privacy,
> data from the phishing simulations and the survey were linked using anonymised, hashed
> identifiers through a protocol which ensured that neither the researchers, nor the IT
> security team had access to any personally identifiable survey responses. The ethics
> application and this data-processing protocol were also reviewed by the participating
> organisation's Chief Information Security Officer and the ISG.

## Points worth drawing out

**The simulations were not caused by the research.** They were part of an existing annual
awareness programme and would have been run regardless. The research used the resulting
logs; it did not induce anyone to be phished.

**Consent differs between the two data sources.** The approved protocol explicitly
permitted the simulations without individual consent, since advance notice would have
destroyed their validity and they were routine practice. The survey was separate:
participation was voluntary and informed consent was obtained from respondents.

**The research team never held identifiable data.** Linkage between simulation logs and
survey responses was performed by the institution's IT Security Group using a hashed
per-participant key, and only the linked pseudonymised result was transferred.

See also `PROVENANCE.md` for how the data was collected and what has been removed.
